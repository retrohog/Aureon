# Oversampling filters and PCM → DSD

## Oversampling filters

`Settings → Audio → Upsampling` (and `PCM to DSD`) offer four filters, all built on the polyphase resampler in `Pipeline/Resampler.swift`:

| Filter | Pass-band | Stop-band | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear phase | adjustable, 0.90–0.99 of Nyquist | 96 / 120 / 144 dB | constant delay, symmetric ringing |
| Minimum phase | adjustable | adjustable | no pre-ringing, delay varies with frequency |
| Apodizing | flat to 0.86 of Nyquist (about 19 kHz for CD) | 130 dB, closed at the source's Nyquist | removes the energy and ringing the recording's own filter left near Nyquist |
| Brickwall | flat to 0.99 of Nyquist | 144 dB | about six times the taps of the standard filter |

Designs are built off the audio thread and cached. A resampler built with a `FilterSwitch` takes a new filter while playing: the design is built on a background queue, and the resampler swaps it in between two reads (20 ms crossfade) or, when the player flushes its buffer after a filter change, at once. The render thread is never involved. A filter that costs more than the Mac can spare at that rate is declined and the player says so.

## PCM → DSD

For a DAC that takes DoP (turn on DoP first). The stream is

1. resampled with the chosen filter to 16 × the base rate (32 × for DSD512): 705.6 kHz, or 1.4112 MHz for DSD512;
2. doubled by Kaiser half-band interpolators (130 dB, 5 to 6 coefficient pairs each) up to the DSD rate;
3. modulated to one bit by a 5th or 7th-order noise-shaping loop;
4. packed 16 bits to each 24-bit DoP sample.

Everything runs on the producer thread in double precision; the ring buffer only ever holds finished DoP words, so the audio thread's rules are untouched. The DSD rate is the highest, up to the setting, that the DAC offers as a DoP rate (DSD512 needs 1,411,200 Hz; 48 kHz material uses the 48 kHz family when the DAC has it) and the Mac can make with 2× headroom (measured at start-up: `PerformanceProfile.modulatorBitsPerSecond`). Stereo only.

**Modulator.** Error-feedback form, transposed direct form II, so the noise-transfer function is exactly `b(z)/a(z)` with a signal transfer function of 1. The coefficients are in `Pipeline/ModulatorTables.swift`, generated by `Tools/modulator/design_ntf.py`: zeros at the Legendre points across the audio band, Butterworth high-pass poles moved until the out-of-band gain is 1.40 (5th order) or 1.35 (7th order), inside Lee's limit of 1.5. `Tools/modulator/check.py` and `check_cascade.py` run the same loop in C and measure it:

| | DSD64 | DSD128 | DSD256 / 512 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5th order | 110 dB | 142 dB | 150 dB or more |
| 7th order | 121 dB | 150 dB | 150 dB or more |

(in-band signal-to-noise for a 0.5-scale tone, 20 Hz to 22 kHz; the 150 dB figures are the limit of the measurement). Full scale PCM is mapped to half the one-bit range, the level DSD itself uses for 0 dB. Inputs are limited to ±0.62, where the loops are stable on tones, square waves and noise; a loop whose quantiser input still grows past 8 is reset and counted (`overloads`).

**Not applied to a DSD stream:** room correction, crossfeed, headphone correction, dither and the equalizer run after the ring and would destroy the bits inside a DoP stream, so the settings screen asks before combining them. Transitions fall back to gapless, as for any DoP stream. The signal path shows the conversion as a processed stage.

## Command line

```
Aureon play --dsd-out 512 --modulator 7 --filter apodizing track.flac
Aureon play --filter brickwall --upsample pow2 track.flac
```
